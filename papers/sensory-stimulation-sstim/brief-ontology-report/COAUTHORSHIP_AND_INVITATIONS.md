# Coauthor strategy and unsent drafts

**Status:** internal editorial suggestions. Nothing here is an accepted invitation, an endorsement, or a submitted email.

## Priorities for the first Brief Ontology Report

The present manuscript has one declared author, Renato Fabbri. This is appropriate **until substantial contributions and explicit approval** justify a different author list.

- **Osvaldo Novais de Oliveira Jr. (“Chu”)**: strong potential scientific contributor for the *formalization of physical stimuli, sensing, uncertainty, signal descriptions and scientific validation*. He was Renato's master's and doctoral adviser. Invite for a focused scientific critique or concrete reworking of the evaluation; ask about coauthorship only if a substantive contribution is anticipated or delivered.
- **Roberto Vezzani**: strong prospect for a later **AI interoperability / multimodal measurement** paper. Invite his technical input on an independent benchmark or AI evaluation; for the first ontology report, coauthor only if he contributes to a real method or evaluation included in it.
- **Cristina Iani**: excellent for a follow-up **experimental sensory/perceptual/cognitive and social-experience** paper. Her critique of sensory/perceptual/cognitive boundaries would be valuable now; mere editorial advice is not automatically grounds for coauthorship.
- **Alberto Levi**: a plausible specialist for a future **workplace, AI governance, consent and human agency** article. His disciplinary expertise is peripheral to the core claims of this first ontology report, so he is not a default coauthor.

**An additional independent applied-ontology specialist** would be at least as valuable as a famous name from an adjacent field, particularly to perform a skeptical alternative-model encoding or review of formal consistency.

Authorship criteria are not a reward for prestige, institutional affiliation, mentorship, introducing a contact, or securing access. Ask people to **contribute to a well-defined scientific question**; discuss coauthorship if actual intellectual design, analysis, implementation, writing and responsibility warrant it. Ask for their consent before listing names in public manuscript metadata.

## Suggested invitation process

1. Send the short report privately with a **specific technical question** and a small, bounded requested contribution.
2. Invite critique without implying an obligation or assuming a pre-agreed coauthor position.
3. If a prospective collaborator wants to contribute substantially, agree on study scope, deliverable, author role and anticipated timeline before expanding the manuscript.
4. Keep the first report narrow; route broader ideas to the second and third papers.
