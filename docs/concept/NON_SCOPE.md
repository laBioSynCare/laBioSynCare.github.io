# Non-Scope

This document defines what the Sensory Stimulation Vocabulary Community Group should not do.

## Not a Clinical Guideline Body

The group does not define clinical practice guidelines.

## Not a Therapeutic Certification Body

The group does not certify therapeutic efficacy.

## Not a Medical-Device Regulator

The group does not evaluate or approve regulated-device claims.

## Not a Public-Health Authority

The group does not issue public-health recommendations.

## Not a Protocol Prescription Body

The group does not prescribe medical protocols, disease-specific treatment protocols, or individual health habits.

## Not a Claim-Validation Authority

The group does not claim that any stimulation protocol treats, cures, prevents, or diagnoses medical conditions.

## Not a BioSynCare Product Standard

BioSynCare/BSC Lab may provide early examples and implementation feedback, but the vocabulary must not be framed as a BioSynCare product specification.

## Not a Replacement for Evidence Review

The vocabulary may describe evidence annotations and claim-strength metadata, but it does not itself establish clinical validity.

## Public and Institutional Relevance Boundary

The group may develop terminology that helps sensory stimulation be communicated more clearly to authorities, institutions, public-interest organizations, researchers, and the public.

The group may describe concepts relevant to public and individual health practices, such as safety metadata, evidence annotations, intended-use statements, accessibility metadata, and claim-strength metadata.

However, the group does not:

- issue public-health recommendations;
- prescribe individual health habits;
- certify protocols as effective;
- define clinical care pathways;
- replace clinical, regulatory, or public-health authorities;
- decide whether a product or intervention should be used for a specific health condition.

## Status Discipline

Community Group work must not be described as a W3C Standard unless it separately
enters and completes an appropriate W3C standards-track process. Never write
"W3C standard", "official standard", "certified by W3C", "therapeutic standard",
or "medical standard".

The permitted wording is in [`CHARTER.md`](../../CHARTER.md#status-discipline)
and is not duplicated here — the copy that was here drifted, still recommending
"Community Group proposal" after the group had launched.
